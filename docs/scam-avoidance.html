What a scam is

A scam is when someone tries to trick you into giving them money, passwords, or personal information. Scammers often pretend to be someone you trust, like a bank, the government, a delivery company, or even a grandchild in trouble.

Common warning signs

Checklist: Spot a scam

  1. They create urgency: "Act now or your account will be closed."
  2. They ask for money by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency.
  3. They ask for your password, Social Security number, or bank account number.
  4. They say you won a prize you never entered to win.
  5. They claim to be a relative in trouble and ask for money in secret.
  6. The message has spelling mistakes, odd wording, or a strange sender address.
  7. They call you out of the blue and claim there is a problem with your computer.

Phone scams

Checklist: Handle a suspicious call

  1. If you do not recognize the number, let it go to voicemail.
  2. Never call back a number left in a voicemail that claims to be your bank or the government.
  3. If you want to call your bank, use the number on the back of your card or on a real statement.
  4. Never give money, passwords, or personal information to an unexpected caller.
  5. Hang up. It is not rude to protect yourself.

Email and text scams

Checklist: Handle a suspicious email or text

  1. Do not click links in messages you did not expect.
  2. Do not open attachments from people you do not know.
  3. Look at the sender's email address closely. Scammers often use addresses that look almost real but have extra letters or odd endings.
  4. When in doubt, contact the company using a phone number or website you already know is real.
  5. Delete the message.

Fake websites

Checklist: Tell if a website is real

  1. Look for a small lock icon next to the website address in your browser.
  2. Check that the address is spelled correctly (for example, paypal.com, not paypa1.com).
  3. Type the address yourself instead of clicking a link in an email.
  4. Never enter passwords or bank details on a site you reached through an unexpected link.

If you think you were scammed

Checklist: What to do

  1. Do not panic. You are not alone, and help is available.
  2. If you gave a bank or credit card number, call your bank right away.
  3. If you gave a password, change it on every account where you used it.
  4. Report it: in the U.S., you can report scams at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
  5. Tell a trusted friend or family member so they can help you.

Remember

You do not have to be tech-savvy to stay safe. If something feels off, pause, hang up, delete the message, or ask someone you trust before you act.

Why passwords matter

A password is like a key to your home. A strong password keeps strangers out of your email, bank, and other accounts. You do not need to memorize complicated codes — you just need good habits and a safe place to keep them.

Making a strong password

Checklist: Create a strong password

  1. Make it long — at least 12 characters. Longer is better.
  2. Mix it up: use capital and lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols.
  3. Do not use your name, birthdate, address, or pet's name.
  4. Do not use the same password for more than one account.
  5. If you write it down, keep the paper in a safe place at home, not on your computer.

Easy trick: use a passphrase

A passphrase is several random words strung together. It is long, hard for computers to guess, and easier for you to remember than a jumble of letters.

Example: Purple-Train-Rainbow-42-Teapot

Using a password manager

A password manager is an app that remembers your passwords for you. You only need to remember one master password to unlock it. Many phones and computers come with one built in.

Checklist: Start using your device's password manager

  1. On iPhone or iPad: open Settings, then tap Passwords.
  2. On Android: open Settings, then tap Google > Passwords.
  3. On a Mac: open System Settings, then tap Passwords.
  4. On Windows: look for Passwords in Settings, or use your browser's saved passwords.
  5. When a website asks to save your password, say Yes if you are on your own device.

Your device may ask you to use your face, fingerprint, or phone passcode to open the password manager. That is normal and keeps it safe.

Two-step verification

Two-step verification (also called two-factor authentication) adds a second lock to your account. Even if someone learns your password, they cannot get in without a second code sent to your phone or email.

Checklist: Turn on two-step verification

  1. Open the account settings for your email or an important website.
  2. Look for Security, Sign-in, or Two-step verification.
  3. Turn it on and follow the prompts.
  4. Choose to receive codes by text message or by an authenticator app.
  5. Save any backup codes the site gives you in a safe place.

Password safety reminder

No real company or government agency will ever call, text, or email you asking for your password. If someone asks for your password, it is a scam. Hang up or delete the message. See Scam Avoidance.
